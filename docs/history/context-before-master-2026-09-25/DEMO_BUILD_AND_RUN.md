# Demo build and live conversation

This describes the implementation on `codex/sales-trainer-flow`, with WP1–WP10 implemented in the brief's specified order and WP11's uploaded/customer question bank, on-demand rehearsal and noise guard in the current working tree. The paid Read is skipped because authorization and cap remain blank. [Actual Creta acceptance](CRETA_ACCEPTANCE.md) and the [issue log](issues/2026-09-19-creta.md) describe the earlier published demo and separate measured outcomes from implementation; the new build flow has not rebuilt that demo or established acoustic quality.

## Build: prepare the knowledge and the reusable demo

1. **Sources.** The Studio stores the original PDFs, selected images, website URLs, language and selected voice. `web/studio/sources.js` calls `server/app.py`; `server/sources.py` preserves extraction evidence and source revisions. Originals are not rewritten.
2. **Readiness.** `server/readiness.py` distinguishes configured credentials from recently observed reasoning, voice and transcription success. Read requires reasoning readiness; Build also requires the selected voice. Probes are explicit and may incur provider charges. An explicit override does not remove the six review approvals.
3. **Read.** `server/graph.py` and `server/orchestrator.py` run the build stages. `server/crawl.py` discovers relevant pages within a model and market boundary. `server/sources.py` extracts text and tables, applies OCR when needed, and reports missing or deferred coverage. It does not promise to read an entire manufacturer's website.
4. **Understand.** `server/agents/understand.py` extracts claims with quotes, locators, applicability and source identity. `server/knowledge.py` reconciles stable assertion IDs, records conflicts and provides retrieval. Uploaded material wins a genuine conflict within the same scope; different trims, markets and dates are not automatically conflicting.
5. **Coach.** `server/agents/coach.py` owns the reviewed category story order, coverage, three supported winning points and evidence gaps. Its versioned library covers compact/premium SUVs, electric scooters and an explicitly inferred generic order. It starts with fundamentals and keeps delighters after them. Unsupported stops remain visible as gaps; available pictures do not decide what the salesperson covers. Code validates evidence identities, stop order and USP names before saving `playbook.json`. Duplicate stops keep their first occurrence; USP count and name errors remain visible issues, with extra USPs truncated and unsafe names cleaned or dropped rather than aborting Read. Cache reuse checks category and consumed picture/evidence inputs as well as registry, library and audience identity. Open customer unknowns become evidence gaps with their question and asked count; they never become facts. Resolving a gap removes it from the active playbook view.
6. **Plan, then author.** `server/agents/plan.py` reads that playbook and saved human overrides before the approved registry. It maps evidence and existing image/shot identities to one proof segment per required stop, preserves reviewed order and stop-first evidence without dropping approved Planner additions, inherits exactly three valid Coach USPs (otherwise retains Planner USPs with an issue), and restores missing required stops with an issue. Each segment carries `stop_id`, `fundamental` and `word_budget`; the plan records its playbook version and a word target derived from `settings.pitch_minutes`. It floors the guided target at three minutes, reserves 23 overview words and 45 closing words, and allocates the remainder to proof/features/establish stops. Browse intro/outcome segments have separate budgets. Allocations stay within role ceilings; infeasible coverage is visible instead of padded. Understand still creates the image tags; Planner selects those identities without retagging or inventing media.

   `SegmentPlan.goal` remains the Author's three-part brief: the moment, spoken-versus-deeper facts and material conditions, then the literal visual and handoff. Goals are an outline, not final dialogue. `PITCH_SHAPE` and `PROOF_BLOCK` belong to planning. No additional audit fields such as `say_as`, `arc` or `narration_fact_ids` are introduced in this work package.

   `author.py` owns connected spoken thoughts, varied openings, sentence rhythm and the short Explore overview. It receives the full goals and editorial `plan.notes`, keeps IDs/roles/order, and follows the settled route. Continuity is a connection between ideas, not a reference that breaks when a proof stop plays alone. The translation ladder requires the highest supported everyday explanation; a technical figure moves intact to deeper detail, while its supported purpose remains in the main tour. A fit-check is a last resort, not filler replacing missing narration. Trim, transmission, purchase and policy conditions stay with their claims. Opening shapes guide variety; they are not stock phrases to repeat. The opening previews the first fundamental without digits or a decision frame.

   The mapped audit also bans company/market statistics as selling points, permitting only one cited reputational line in the intro without a figure or rank. It uses titles that can be spoken naturally and makes repairs local to the diagnosed segments. Prompts place at most two one-line closing statements where a decision turns. The `checkin` field carries a statement, never a question; validation flags question marks. Playback speaks closing statements and continues without a response gate; older question-shaped check-ins retain source text/audio but are skipped, with `checkin_skipped: "legacy_question"` saved on the run. Images establish appearance, not unmeasured comfort or performance; missing literal images stay explicit. Grounding validators remain unchanged and do not prove arbitrary semantic entailment.

   Author checks the planned budget as well as the role ceiling. More than four words over budget is an issue; more than ten under an explicit allocation invites an advisory warning about a missing join or moment. Legacy scripts without typed allocations keep ceiling-based upper checks and receive no new under-budget warning. Ceilings are 46 words for intro/outcome/proof, 48 for features and 44 for establish, with 45 for the closing. The route limit derives from the configured pitch minutes, floored at three, plus a forty-word allowance. New drafts also surface the default narration deficit to the existing single Author repair; only distinct supported material can close that deficit. These are word checks, not a guarantee that recorded speech fits twenty seconds. Longer batches split only at existing line boundaries above the role ceiling plus four; their planned allocation must not be duplicated across continuation batches.
   Before Align, `deck.py` assembles one slide per segment with up to two pictures. It takes the first two distinct image bindings in verified line order, requiring full audit coverage when an audit exists. Each picture records the first line it accompanies. If no picture qualifies, a trusted nearby part tag can supply an illustration (engine → bonnet/front, for example); otherwise the hero illustrates the cited fact. The proxy badge and reason remain explicit, and this choice never rewrites the pixel audit or counts as proof. Each picture carries its own grounded callouts and trusted anchors; uncertain anchors remain in that picture's panel.
   `faq.py` answers only questions extracted from uploaded documents during Read. It does not generate likely questions or fill a configured quota. Eligible customer answers and rejected-entry tombstones survive rebuilds; a policy marker triggers one reconciliation of older generated banks. When there are no active entries or open customer unknowns, Read automatically approves the Asked and answered card with the note **“No questions yet; this card fills from customer questions”**.

7. **Align.** `web/studio/align.js` presents the same six card identities: facts, visuals, script, Asked and answered (`faq`), persona and CTAs. The Script card's Story order panel shows stops, kinds, evidence/picture counts and gaps. Saving order/kinds writes overrides, marks downstream work stale, resets Script/Visuals approval and revises Plan; each gap can request its missing source through the existing action. The slide editor provides a picker for each picture and retains label coordinates relative to that picture. The save API rejects unknown/excluded media or more than two pictures. Illustration badges remain visible. Visuals also offers Marine, Sage and Graphite; changing the color choice resets Visuals approval and marks the bundle stale without changing source pictures or proof coordinates.

   Timing compares each segment's planned seconds with its main narration, and compares the planned total with full content duration and configured `pitch_minutes`. Full content includes spoken check-ins and closing. A word-based duration remains an estimate. The Script card separately shows the default guided narration duration, missing seconds and measured/estimated basis; publication requires complete recorded audio and at least 180 seconds. The existing timeline `exact` flag means some main-line audio was measured; it alone cannot label the whole timeline measured. A segment's measured narration label requires recordings for all its verified main lines. Customer waiting and Q&A time are outside this content timeline. Meaningful fact edits create new assertion IDs; old published snapshots remain unchanged. Rejected or changed evidence invalidates downstream approvals. `server/agents/align.py` handles conversation-based edits. Asked and answered shows uploaded answers, customer answers, asked counts and customer unknowns. Operators can approve, edit or reject an answer through the existing review route. Editing preserves the reviewed words and clears an old clip when the text changes; rejection persists as a tombstone so the answer cannot be served or regenerated into the bank. Material content changes reset the card approval; asked-count and audio-only changes do not.
8. **Build.** `voice.py` records the selected guide voice; `speech_style.py` supplies plain speech text and restrained pace. Tone is writing guidance, not an unsupported vendor emotion control. `bundle.py` first checks that the actual playable default route, including its opening and closing, contains at least 180 seconds of distinct supported recorded narration in every published language. Film, customer Q&A and deeper-only lines do not count. Missing or unreadable clips and underlength speech block publication before the snapshot, bundle or version changes; the old publication stays intact. Browser speech estimates cannot pass this gate. After it passes, Bundle publishes a version tied to an immutable knowledge snapshot. Build does not run rehearsal or invent questions. The bundle resolves both picture URLs and picture-owned callouts, while keeping first-picture `image_id`/`image_url` for older readers. All six approvals are required.

The Rehearse screen has an explicit **Run rehearsal** action. It reviews saved customer questions first, then uploaded document questions. Old-registry answers are checked against current evidence; only an empty bank permits up to five sample questions. It records coverage and the script scorecard in `rehearsal.json` without creating customer questions or facts, changing approvals, or republishing the bundle. The operation is serialized with other build work and preserves the ready state.

Build progress uses durable server-sent events in `server/events.py` and `web/api.js`. Reconnecting resumes from the event cursor. This is separate from the customer's live conversation connection.

The 2026-09-19 role-split exercise produced `output/creta-night-final/plan.session.json` and `script.session.json` using the agents' existing session tokens, approved v7 assertions and inspected original images. It made no paid Plan/Author/Build provider call. At independent candidate review, the script had null audio and remained separate from publication; its estimated timing was not measured speech. Provenance and semantic checks are in the adjacent `plan.semantic-review.json`, `script.session.review.json` and `author-independent-review.json`. Human Align, publication and any separately authorized final stress session keep their existing gates; candidate review is not live or acoustic acceptance.

## Run: a conversation over reviewed evidence

The new runtime **does use an LLM**. The prepared narration and FAQ remain useful build artifacts, but a live question first checks an eligible saved answer and otherwise goes through a conversation graph. Legacy REST QA callers without a runtime session retain their earlier contract.

`web/player/player.js` owns the customer's experience: the current slide, narration position, question wait, captions and resumption point. `live-voice.js` owns the WebSocket and microphone lifecycle; `voice-worklet.js` supplies PCM frames. The welcome **Voice mode** switch defaults on only for continuous-voice bundles without `?mute=1`. Explore and Browse use that selection: off opens the socket for streamed guide speech without requesting microphone access; on requests capture once and keeps it across turns after permission is granted. The player mic button turns capture on or off without reconnecting the socket, and the typed field stays available in both modes. Capture stays active until switched off, stopped or the session ends. Permission denial leaves typing available; a browser cannot grant permission automatically.

`web/slide.js` fits two pictures side by side at stage widths of at least 700 px and stacks them below that, preserving each image's native aspect. As audio advances the line index, `setRevealed` emphasizes the latest eligible picture and dims the other to0.55 without moving either. Callouts reveal within their owning picture, and a proxy retains its **illustration** badge. The player still follows audio events; picture changes do not introduce playback timers.

The intake also offers **Websites I can check (optional)** below its text input. The spoken intake remains one question. The client retains up to five parsed sites and appends URLs supplied in later customer turns. The server merges profile URLs, the previous checkpoint and the current turn into a deduplicated session set of at most eight. These URLs authorize bounded live lookup; they are not approved product facts. `settings.runtime_default_sites` defaults to `off`; when explicitly enabled, only enabled product URL source rows join the session's sites.

For a question or interruption:

1. Raw microphone level spikes and provider VAD events provide timing candidates but do not stop narration. The first meaningful partial or final transcript confirms customer speech and interrupts playback; empty text, punctuation and noise annotations do not. Explicit customer stop controls still cancel immediately. Session, turn, utterance and capture-generation IDs prevent stale audio or transcripts from taking over a newer turn. These mocked guards do not establish physical microphone quality or measured interruption latency.
2. Final transcription submits one customer turn. After a 250 ms grace period, the shortest compatible reviewed acknowledgement may play while work is pending. Creta uses “Let me check that.” in Priya's voice. A ready answer can cut the acknowledgement short.
3. `server/runtime_live.py` routes the turn to `runtime_graph.py`; `runtime_state.py` owns the deadline, cancellation and checkpoint. Before graph reasoning, `faq.match` checks the same pinned snapshot and registry hash, approved citations and rejection state. A hit returns exact saved words with `from_bank: true`, increments `asked_count`, and uses its existing clip when available; `skip_bank` bypasses this path. Otherwise retrieval uses the session's published knowledge snapshot plus explicitly stated scope and recent conversation context.
4. An explicit request to check a supplied URL dispatches the bounded lookup first, saving a model round and preventing a saved fact from impersonating a fresh page check. With empty retrieved evidence and saved customer sites, the graph also requests one relevant allowed site before the first model call. A round-zero decline gets the same opportunity if no lookup has run. Interaction acts do not trigger automatic browsing. The reasoner receives the session URL union as `CUSTOMER_URLS`; otherwise it chooses a grounded answer, one necessary clarification, or a tool request. `runtime_tools.py` offers a Decimal calculator and bounded lookup of allowed session sites. No arbitrary agent code execution is available.
5. Tool results return to the LLM for the final response. The graph allows at most two tool rounds and four tool calls within one twelve-second deadline. Model composition leaves250ms inside that deadline for validation and delivery. If composition fails after a successful audited calculation, the graph can deliver that completed result with its assumptions; a pending/failed tool or cancelled turn cannot qualify. `reasoning_failed` and `calculation_fallback` preserve the distinction between failed wording generation and successful arithmetic delivery. Quoted inputs, units and assumptions accompany a calculation. EMI is illustrative, not a lender offer. Web failures remain failures; the agent cannot claim to have checked an inaccessible page.
6. **Validate, then prepare delivery.** `runtime_graph.py` checks citations, quantities, scope, policy conditions and tool provenance. `runtime_facts.py` checks equipment combinations and literal trim ranges; an exact list cannot authorize “and above”. `runtime_coverage.py` rejects whole-source absence or exhaustive-content claims inferred from selected passages. `runtime_tables.py` requires each universal live-table feature to match its own row, with aligned complete headers, equal trim sets and explicit positive status meanings. Missing legends and conditional marks cannot prove universal fitment; unfamiliar phrasing is conservatively rejected. This applies to cited facts, context and limitations. These are deterministic checks, not a general semantic proof.

   `runtime_acts.py` supplies fixed wording for question-relevant own limits, seating checks and missing inputs. This includes current dealer-delivery uncertainty and a boundary against fabricated evidence; it never echoes a proposed fake number or citation. These acts cannot carry product facts or citations. The graph keeps one question last and waits on the current slide. A separately validated refusal can precede it; raw later model text cannot bypass validation. Legacy text-only interactions remain supported by `runtime_interaction.py`.

   If the answer is empty or useful content is rejected, one optional composition repair uses the same evidence and specific feedback. It cannot call tools, add a CTA, extend the deadline or override cancellation. Every repaired sentence goes through validation again. A failed repair retains useful original facts. A partly valid repair can add checked facts or a precise limit only under the preservation rules; rejected clauses and errors remain visible. `repair_failed` distinguishes this optional call from a whole-turn failure.

   `runtime_emi_delivery.py` appends only missing audited EMI assumptions and fee/tax/non-lender qualifications. It does not silently correct a wrong model number. The final graph output is a **delivery plan**; replayable graph nodes do not themselves speak. A clean validated answer over approved facts can then enter the customer bank with its snapshot/hash, `source: customer` and `reviewed: false`. Clarifications, provider or repair failures, live-web answers and calculations are not cached. A supported decline or “I won't guess” becomes a deduplicated customer unknown, with count and session identity, rather than a fact or cached answer. Plain-language substitutions precede storage.
7. `runtime_delivery.py` and `llm/sarvam_stream.py` stream the selected voice. The browser owns playback and captions. A fully completed answer recording can be saved through the existing text-hash cache and attached only to the same entry with exactly matching words; a late clip cannot overwrite an edited answer. A runtime-v1 question jump shows the matching evidence and begins the answer directly, without a separate generic transition sentence. The guide's clarification waits for a real reply. After an answer or decline finishes, chips remain available and a three-second reply window opens; typing is focused and voice-mode capture remains open. Silence then resumes the saved narration position, replaying an interrupted line from its start. Confirmed speech, a typed character or a chip action cancels that timer. Cancelled or superseded output and timers cannot advance a newer turn.

   Automatic return uses `back_to_demo` only when an existing recorded clip is available; otherwise it resumes silently. Saved turns record `auto_resumed` and the listen-window duration. Stop-closing check-ins never open a reply wait. Closing choices, CTAs and lead forms continue to require customer action; the timer never submits a lead or chooses a CTA.

For **Explore with me**, the customer's context starts personalization while the recorded, cited overview plays. `server/agents/pitch.py` reorders relevant unseen segments and composes exact reviewed factual sentences, with at most one short customer-context preface on the first slide. It does not freely rewrite unverified product claims. The overview leads directly into that slide speech, without an additional decision-frame or custom-batch opening. If model replacements are missing or invalid, the selected route uses explicitly marked reviewed narration and retains its recorded clips and check-ins. A changed priority can replan unseen material at a safe boundary. A slow or failed plan falls back honestly instead of claiming customization succeeded.

The reviewed `fundamental` flag travels from segment to Slide, bundle and pitch library. For a new minimum-checked publication, the default Explore route begins with the first fundamental and extends the buyer-priority order beyond three proof stops until measured narration reaches 180 seconds. A slide viewed during pre-tour Q&A is not completed narration. The player retains complete reviewed lines and closing speech; shorter personalized subsets fall back to the complete reviewed narration. Explicitly requested short tours, skips/exits and later refinements remain customer choices. Legacy publications retain their existing seen-stop policy. The initial player fallback keeps the complete reviewed route.

Live personalization reads the published bundle, not mutable Align drafts. A session pins both the knowledge snapshot and published demo version. A newer publication requires a refresh before new personalization, even if the facts have not changed. Existing Q&A stays grounded in the pinned knowledge snapshot.

## How evidence, tools and the LLM fit together

Retrieval currently uses local lexical and feature similarity over scoped evidence; it is not an external vector database. The model receives compact approved claims, values, conditions and scope. Conditions can be reviewed applicability caveats; they must not be presented as verbatim manufacturer statements without the original wording. A large original source quote is retained for audit, but its unrelated table cells do not become additional licensed claims. Exact negative applicability can explain an explicitly excluded trim; a missing retrieved fact does not prove that the entire source corpus lacks that feature. A trim found only in a source-specific listing does not inherit another source’s universal equipment. Literal and same-revision facts can remain useful with explicit attribution; uncertain lineage stays uncertain. Negative, excluded and incompatible old-market/year assertions cannot establish current trim identity. Resolved model and trim names filter scope but do not dominate topic ranking. Explicit comfort queries expand to seating, ventilation, climate and recline terms at query time; the same snapshot, eligibility filters and14-result limit still apply. Compatible supplied evidence can also carry a mandatory device-purchase prerequisite across duplicate feature assertions: switching citations cannot hide the Alexa device purchase. Primary claim citations and condition-only donor citations stay separate in the result and player. A donor does not license its other product details or trim fitment. This is narrow English screening, not a general semantic proof or a multilingual acceptance claim.

Uncited lines are restricted to bounded interaction, exact attributed customer input and the agent’s own specific verification/input limits. They cannot announce unsupported world facts such as an unpublished future lineup.

For an everyday audience, grounded answer rows then pass the shared `plain_terms.py` check before the word limit. Longest whole-phrase matching converts reviewed engineering names to neutral ordinary wording, preserving their citations and conditions. For example, “McPherson strut” becomes “strut-type front suspension”; the mapping supplies no extra benefit claim. Common terms such as turbo, gearbox and suspension remain usable. An unresolved blocked term rejects the sentence with a specific repair instruction under the existing repair/deadline limit. An explicit technical-specification question or expert audience may retain the exact sourced term. The map is finite and is not a general language-quality guarantee.

FAQ builds use the same everyday substitutions; narration receives warnings from the shared blocked-term list. Cached and legacy answer paths preserve the same terminology behavior and discard old audio when wording changes. `plain_language_substitutions` remains attached to the result and saved player turn so review can distinguish the rendered words from the original technical wording.

The LLM drafts conversational language, normally in up to three short sentences/75words. An explicit list or comparison can use a fourth sentence and100words to preserve requested facets and conditions. This is an instruction, not proof of complete answers. The price validator preserves an explicitly stated price-change caveat; it does not verify a saved price as current. Currency notation and requested-page attribution are checked independently. Tools perform arithmetic and fetch a supplied public source. A verified calculator result retains its derivation and can provide a deterministic response when model rendering fails; approximate currency wording permits bounded rounding. Validators and reviewed evidence constrain the answer. None of those layers alone proves that every possible answer is correct; the real-question acceptance pack tests their combined behavior.

Live URL lookup is deliberately smaller than build-time crawling: up to three relevant same-host, model/market-scoped pages from an allowed session site within a bounded fetch budget. Recognized `price-in-<city>` child pages require a locality named by the customer or an explicitly supplied city URL; a page menu or generated query cannot choose the customer’s city. This narrow rule does not infer residence or resolve arbitrary city aliases. It does not silently refresh the approved knowledge base. New web evidence remains attached to that turn with its source and applicability caveats. A cited child page cannot impersonate the exact requested page. Live claims require host attribution, and wording such as “according to … page” or “as per … website” cannot make a stored fact look freshly checked. Private-network destinations and unsafe redirects are rejected.

Source approval is consequential: a model can accurately repeat a wrong approved assertion. The CRETA review found this in a compiled guide's turbo displacement. Reviewed corrections now include a narrow cross-label cc/cm³ conflict check, plus explicit source review. Source changes publish a new snapshot; they never rewrite old evidence or silently retarget a running session.

## Response metrics and their limits

`server/runtime_metrics.py` aggregates session events; `web/observability.js` displays them. Compare the same demo version, voice, input mode, route and speech-end measurement basis. Saved sessions carry `input_mode: "voice" | "text"`; actual turn input remains authoritative, so a typed question within a voice-mode visit stays in the typed cohort. Typed input and realtime voice are separate automatic cohorts. Synthetic voice and human microphone sessions must additionally be distinguished through acceptance-run labels; they share the realtime input mode and are not automatically separated. Latency calculations use browser timestamps; a server endpoint timestamp is retained separately so clock skew cannot inflate or shrink response time.

Answer, clarification and decline are separate response cohorts. Historical unclassified turns remain explicitly labelled legacy data. A quick decline cannot improve answer-playback percentiles, and `answered=true` is not a semantic quality grade. Actual useful-answer quality is reviewed separately against evidence.

- **Endpoint delay:** estimated end of customer speech to endpoint-event receipt. Final-transcript receipt is recorded separately; this metric is not transcription completion.
- **Response start:** customer speech end or typed submission to actual first answer audio. An acknowledgement is not the substantive answer.
- **Reasoning and tools:** retrieval, model, tool, optional repair and total graph timings. Deterministic validation has no separate timer.
- **Completion:** submission or speech end to completed answer playback. Clarifications are excluded because their completion timestamp includes waiting for the customer.
- **Interruption:** detected speech onset to the application's local stop request. This excludes detection delay and does not measure speaker echo or physical silence.
- **Reliability:** failed and cancelled turns remain visible in counts and are excluded from successful latency percentiles. Missing measurements are absent, never zero. Small cohorts are labelled.

The configured text order remains Gemini → Claude → Runware. An explicit Anthropic insufficient-credit400 starts a60-second process-local cooldown for that effective credential/endpoint/model. Later budgeted turns trace the skip and proceed to the next provider; expiry or an effective identity change permits retry. Ordinary timeouts, rate limits and other errors do not start this cooldown. Legacy calls without a turn deadline keep their original behavior. This avoids repeated known credit failures; it does not guarantee fallback availability.

An experimental `RUNTIME_HEDGE_ENABLED=1` path starts one fallback lane after a configured delay while allowing Gemini its existing seven-second cap. `RUNTIME_HEDGE_DELAY_S` defaults to five seconds and accepts only finite values from one to five; health reports the effective value. A three-second trial gives fallback more time but can add paid losing work, including during short repair calls. It is **off by default** and only applies to Gemini-first calls with sufficient remaining time and an explicit turn-cancellation token. Both lanes share the same deadline; the first schema-valid result proceeds to the ordinary grounding guard. Each provider is attempted at most once by the dispatcher. Returned late usage remains attributed, while late results and new repair/fallback launches are discarded. Launch telemetry records preparation intent, not proof of a provider request. A later stop or insufficient-budget check emits `skipped_before_dispatch` with `dispatched=false`; attempts that actually reach an adapter retain their existing completion/failure/late-result events. Already-sent work cannot be refunded or forcibly stopped by cancelling a thread. A same-code100-case observational comparison completed: fallback recovery and late accounting worked, but aggregate latency and usefulness did not improve. Provider conditions differed, so no causal performance claim is warranted. Keep it off by default.

A full reasoning outage uses a fixed operational message from code and does not masquerade as a missing knowledge-base fact. A completed audited calculator result can still be delivered, with `reasoning_failed=true`, `calculation_fallback=true`, and `provider_failed=false`. Reasoning time accumulates across tool rounds, including a failed final round.

The twelve-second graph deadline is a configured bound, **not an observed response-time KPI**. Actual latency and failure rates must come from the acceptance report. Muted playback can validate timing, captions, state transitions and audio bytes; human listening, echo cancellation and perceived naturalness still require an audible microphone session.
